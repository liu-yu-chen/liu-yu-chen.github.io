## Research focus

How have application topics, modeled agents, LLM use, and collaboration changed in agent-based modeling (ABM)? The supplied review manuscript combines an indexed-literature corpus with topic, trend, and coauthorship analyses.

## Corpus and analytical design

The strict analytical corpus contains **29,139 English research publications dated 1972–2025**, assembled from Web of Science, PubMed, and DBLP and enriched with OpenAlex abstracts. Titles and abstracts were classified for application areas, agent types, LLM use and its role, and collaboration patterns. The review combines trend tests, regression analyses, and coauthorship-community detection.

The manuscript distinguishes the strict analyzed corpus from a broader set of records. Its filters excluded records without abstracts after enrichment, non-research publication types, and non-English publications. It therefore describes the analyzed corpus, not a complete census of every ABM paper.

## Results in the supplied manuscript

The review reports a shift from a long history of ecology, populations, and disease toward more work on social networks, information diffusion, transport, urban systems, and socio-technical settings.

LLM adoption remained an early-stage share of the corpus but increased year by year: **1.50% in 2023, 2.75% in 2024, and 5.65% in 2025**. The authors report a Cochran–Armitage trend test of **p < 0.001**. The number of papers classified as LLM-related rose from 32 to 63 to 141 over those years.

The review classified the LLM role for **235 papers**: **140 (59.6%)** used an LLM in agent behavior or decisions, while **95 (40.4%)** used it for supporting tasks such as implementation, data, analysis, or interfaces. One additional paper in the 236-paper LLM set did not have a classifiable role.

## Interpretation and limits

The results describe patterns in titles, abstracts, and the selected publication databases. Automated classification and database coverage shape the findings. LLM adoption estimates cover only 2023–2025; the manuscript presents these as early diffusion, not mature evidence that LLM-enabled agents are accurate, valid, or reproducible.
